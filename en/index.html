#01 Stonehenge 巨石阵

Location：英国威尔特郡　Date：约公元前 3000–1600 年（分期建造）

The Mystery：Built in multiple phases, Stonehenge's heaviest sarsen stones weigh about 25 tonnes, while its "bluestones" came from the Preseli Hills of Wales, 225 km away. How Late Neolithic people moved these blocks over such distances and raised them in precise mortise-and-tenon joints — without wheeled vehicles or metal tools — remains unresolved. The monument's main axis aligns with the midsummer sunrise; whether it served as an observatory, a burial ground, a healing sanctuary, or a place of ancestor worship has been debated for over a century. A 2024 study further traced the central Altar Stone to northeastern Scotland, some 750 km away, adding a new transport puzzle.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A multi-phase ritual and funerary monument complex; the solstitial alignments served a seasonal calendar and ancestor cult, reflecting the complex social organisation of Late Neolithic society.

Open Controversy：No consensus on how the stones were moved (overland, by water, or by glacier); the monument's exact function remains an open question.

#02 Avebury 阿维伯里

Location：英国威尔特郡　Date：约公元前 2850–2200 年

The Mystery：Avebury is one of Europe's largest Neolithic stone circles, its outer ring more than 300 metres across with nearly a hundred original stones — far larger than Stonehenge, yet long overlooked. Its exact purpose is unknown: an astronomical observatory, a religious ritual centre, or a ground model of a lost cosmology? Medieval villagers toppled and buried many of the stones, leaving the original layout incomplete. Together with nearby Silbury Hill and West Kennet Long Barrow it forms a vast ritual landscape that suggests a unified plan — but the planners' intent can no longer be recovered.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The core of a Neolithic ritual landscape, part of a long-used ceremonial and funerary complex together with the surrounding barrows and mounds, probably linked to ancestor worship and seasonal rites.

Open Controversy：The circle's specific function and building sequence are hard to establish; medieval destruction makes a full reconstruction of the original layout impossible.

#03 Newgrange 纽格莱奇墓

Location：爱尔兰米斯郡（博因谷）　Date：约公元前 3200 年

The Mystery：This roughly 5,200-year-old passage tomb predates Stonehenge by about a thousand years and the Great Pyramid of Giza by about five hundred. Each winter solstice, a beam of sunlight passes through the "roof-box" above the entrance and travels 19 metres down the passage into the chamber's depths for about 17 minutes — whether this precise alignment was deliberate design or coincidence is still disputed. The entrance stone and chamber slabs are carved with spirals, triskele and other megalithic art whose symbolism is undeciphered. The white quartz façade, rebuilt in the 1970s, has also long been questioned for its fidelity to the original.

Mainstream Archaeological View：An elite burial and ritual monument of a Neolithic farming society, the solstice illumination linked to ancestor worship and seasonal rites.

Open Controversy：The white quartz wall is a modern reconstruction of disputed authenticity; the spiral motifs have no generally accepted interpretation.

#04 Carnac stones 卡纳克巨石阵

Location：法国布列塔尼　Date：约公元前 4600–3300 年（2025 年发表于《Antiquity》的最新测年显示，部分石列可早至公元前 4600–4300 年，为欧洲最早的巨石建筑之一）

The Mystery：Stretching some 10 kilometres across the Carnac region, more than 3,000 standing stones stand in over a dozen rows — the largest prehistoric stone-row complex in the world, more than a millennium older than Stonehenge. Why expend such enormous labour to line thousands of stones in straight rows? Astronomical sight-lines, pilgrimage procession ways, burial boundaries, or a ground-level expression of a lost cosmic order? The builders left no writing, so every explanation rests on indirect evidence; the rows' true purpose remains a mystery.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual landscape built up over a long Neolithic period, possibly connected with ancestor worship, territorial marking, or communal ceremony.

Open Controversy：The rows' precise purpose cannot be established; popular ideas such as a "seismic array" or "giant observatory" lack evidential support.

#05 Callanish Stones 卡拉尼什巨石阵

Location：苏格兰刘易斯岛　Date：约公元前 2900–2600 年（新石器时代晚期）

The Mystery：The Callanish stones on the Isle of Lewis form a rare cruciform plan: a central monolith 4.8 metres tall ringed by 13 stones, with four rows of stones radiating outward. Organising such an enterprise on a remote island is itself a puzzle of social organisation. Most controversial is the "astronomical alignment" claim: the rows are said to point to the extreme rising and setting positions of the Moon at the "major lunar standstill" of its 18.6-year cycle, making Callanish a prehistoric lunar observatory — but critics argue the alignments may be cherry-picked coincidences, and the monument's true function remains unexplained.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual centre in long use from the Late Neolithic into the Bronze Age; the cruciform plan may echo celestial movements or a cosmology, and burials within the circle point to a funerary role.

Open Controversy：The lunar-alignment theory is popular but rests largely on statistical inference, without decisive confirmation; the specific rituals performed there are unrecoverable.

#06 Maltese Megalithic Temples 马耳他巨石神殿

Location：马耳他（Ġgantija 神殿位于戈佐岛，Ħaġar Qim 神殿位于马耳他主岛西南海岸）　Date：约公元前 3600–2500 年（Ġgantija 神殿约建于公元前 3600 年，早于埃及金字塔与英国巨石阵）

The Mystery：The Maltese islands hold seven megalithic temple sites (a World Heritage listing), built of stones weighing tens of tonnes. How the builders quarried, transported, and raised these blocks without metal tools or the wheel has never been fully reconstructed. Ħaġar Qim shows precise solar alignments: at the summer and winter solstices, sunlight passes through a specific doorway into the inner apse. Stranger still, the "temple builder" culture ended abruptly around 2500 BCE, followed by centuries with almost no human occupation — for reasons unknown.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Religious ritual centres of Neolithic farming communities, built with stone-ball rollers, levers, ramps, and massed human labour; the culture's interruption may relate to drought, soil degradation, or social upheaval.

Open Controversy：Whether the scale of the blocks and the precision of some stonework exceed Neolithic technical limits is debated; the cause of the culture's abrupt disappearance — plague, climate shock, or invasion — is unresolved.

#07 Skara Brae 斯卡拉布雷

Location：英国苏格兰奥克尼群岛梅恩兰岛　Date：约公元前 3100–2500 年（新石器时代）

The Mystery：Europe's best-preserved Neolithic village, known as "the Scottish Pompeii". In 1850 a great storm stripped away the covering sand dunes and revealed eight stone houses. Their furnishings are astonishingly intact: stone beds, dressers, and stools, plus a drainage system and what appear to be indoor toilets — a far more sophisticated way of life than the popular image of "primitive man". Around 2500 BCE the inhabitants suddenly left, for reasons unknown; the settlement's regular layout and mature stonework hardly look like beginners' work.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A farming-and-fishing community settlement, built from locally cleaved stone slabs; abandonment may relate to cooling climate, sandstorm encroachment, or changes in subsistence.

Open Controversy：No consensus on whether the departure was voluntary migration or forced flight (perhaps from a catastrophic storm); whether the village's uniform planning implies organisation above the tribal level is still discussed.

#08 Teotihuacan 特奥蒂瓦坎

Location：墨西哥墨西哥州，今墨西哥城东北约 40 公里　Date：约公元前 100 年–公元 750 年（鼎盛期约公元 450 年，人口达 10 万–20 万）

The Mystery：One of the largest ancient cities of the Americas, Teotihuacan's Pyramids of the Sun and Moon and its "Avenue of the Dead" form a strict gridded city plan aligned to astronomical directions. Yet which people built it remains unanswered — the Aztecs found it already in ruins and called it "the place where the gods were born"; they were not its builders. Around 750 CE the temples and palaces of the city centre were systematically burned — invasion or internal revolt, the evidence is inconclusive. Liquid mercury found in a recently discovered tunnel beneath the Pyramid of the Moon is equally unexplained.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A multi-ethnic city-state, the religious and trading hub of Mesoamerica; its decline is linked to 6th–8th-century droughts, elite infighting, and the defection of subordinate cities.

Open Controversy：The builders' ethnic identity (Toltec, Totonac, and other hypotheses) is unresolved; the nature of the burning event and the purpose of the mercury in the tunnel (some scholars suggest a symbolic "underworld water") are still debated.

#09 Tikal 蒂卡尔

Location：危地马拉佩滕省热带雨林　Date：约公元前 400 年–公元 900 年（古典期玛雅城邦，鼎盛于 8 世纪）

The Mystery：One of the most powerful Classic Maya city-states, Tikal's Great Plaza is ringed by stepped pyramids including the 47-metre-tall Temple I. In the 9th century Tikal, along with the whole Classic Maya civilization, fell into the "Great Collapse"; the city was abandoned and swallowed by jungle for a millennium, until its rediscovery in the 19th century. Tikal waged a century-long hegemonic war with Calakmul 130 km away, with fortunes reversing repeatedly; in 378 CE a mysterious "arrival of strangers", possibly linked to Teotihuacan, rewrote Tikal's dynastic sequence. Structures such as the E-Group show precise solstitial alignments.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A political and religious centre of a Maya city-state, with 60,000–90,000 inhabitants at its peak; the abandonment resulted from a combination of 9th-century drought, chronic warfare, overpopulation, and political breakdown.

Open Controversy：Whether the "Collapse" had a single cause or many has been debated for decades; the nature of the 378 CE "arrival" — a Teotihuacan military expedition or a dynastic marriage — remains undecided.

#10 Sacsayhuamán 萨克塞华曼

Location：秘鲁库斯科城北山丘　Date：约 15 世纪（印加帝国时期，图帕克·印加·尤潘基统治时期始建）

The Mystery：The Inca fortress above Cusco: three zigzagging walls built of polygonal blocks weighing over a hundred tonnes, fitted so tightly that a knife blade cannot pass between them — all without mortar. The Inca had no wheels, no iron tools, and no large draft animals; how these blocks were quarried kilometres away, transported, cut into irregular polygons, and fitted seamlessly remains a classic engineering puzzle, with the zigzag plan itself suspected of anti-seismic design. In 1536 it was the main Inca stronghold during the siege of the Spanish.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A military and religious fortress built by the Inca empire with tens of thousands of labourers using stone and bronze tools, ropes, ramps, levers, and human power; modern reconstruction experiments show that trial-fitting and grinding can achieve seamless joints.

Open Controversy：Popular writers and some researchers argue the workmanship exceeds Inca tooling, suggesting earlier foundations or lost techniques; archaeologists maintain the existing evidence is fully explainable by Inca labour organisation — the two sides remain at odds.

#11 Baalbek 巴勒贝克

Location：黎巴嫩贝卡谷地　Date：罗马时期（朱庇特神庙约公元 1 世纪）；巨石基座年代有争议

The Mystery：The foundations of Baalbek's Temple of Jupiter carry three enormous blocks — the Trilithon — each about 19–20 metres long and weighing an estimated 800–1,000 tonnes, among the largest stones ever moved by human hands. Nearby quarries hold even bigger ones: the "Stone of the Pregnant Woman" (about 1,000 tonnes) and an unnamed block estimated at 1,650 tonnes. Moving, transporting, and setting thousand-tonne blocks with Roman-era lifting machines and levers has never been replicated; and the megalithic foundation's masonry style differs markedly from the Roman stonework above, leading some to suspect the base belongs to an earlier, unknown building phase.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The Temple of Jupiter is a Roman imperial expansion of an earlier Phoenician/Greek sanctuary; the giant blocks demonstrate Roman engineering, moved by human labour, levers, rollers, and earthen ramps.

Open Controversy：The feasibility of thousand-tonne transport with Roman technology has never been verified by replication; the stylistic break between the foundation megaliths and the Roman work leads some researchers to date the base earlier — its age undetermined.

#12 Göbekli Tepe 哥贝克力石阵

Location：土耳其东南部尚勒乌尔法附近　Date：约公元前 9600–前 8200 年（前陶新石器时代）

The Mystery：Around 12,000 years ago, before farming, a group of hunter-gatherers raised on a stone hill a complex of circular enclosures ringed by dozens of T-shaped pillars: multi-tonne stones carved with foxes, snakes, vultures, scorpions, and other animals. This predates writing, the wheel, and metal by millennia, and the pyramids by about 7,000 years. It upends the traditional sequence of "agriculture first, then complex society, then religious architecture" — what social organisation let hunter-gatherers sustain such quarrying, transport, and carving? Around 8000 BCE the site was deliberately backfilled, for motives still unknown.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual site built by pre-pottery Neolithic hunter-gatherer communities, proving complex ritual and cooperation could precede agriculture — "belief before farming".

Open Controversy：The fine carving technique, the scale of organisation required, and the reason for the deliberate burial remain unresolved; popular culture links it to "lost advanced civilizations", but there is no archaeological support.

#13 Derinkuyu 德林库尤地下城

Location：土耳其卡帕多西亚内夫谢希尔省　Date：约公元前 8–7 世纪始建（弗里几亚时期），拜占庭时期大规模扩建

The Mystery：Beneath the Cappadocian tuff lies a vertical city some 60 metres deep with 18 levels, able to house tens of thousands of people and livestock, complete with ventilation shafts, granaries, wineries, schools, churches, tombs, and massive stone doors that could be sealed from inside. Hardest to explain is the ventilation system: dozens of shafts piercing tens of metres of rock to carry air and water to the depths — an astonishing feat of precision and scale under ancient technical conditions. Tunnels run for kilometres to the neighbouring underground city of Kaymakli. Who dug it, when, and with what tools has no certain answer.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A refuge city begun in Phrygian times and repeatedly expanded by Byzantine Christians to escape war and persecution; the shafts and tunnels were designed for long-term shelter.

Open Controversy：Direct evidence for the identity and date of the original diggers is lacking, with some views pushing the origins earlier; how the long inter-city tunnels were cut and the ventilation shafts engineered remains a puzzle of engineering history.

#14 Petra 佩特拉

Location：约旦南部马安省　Date：纳巴泰王国鼎盛期，约公元前 1 世纪–公元 1 世纪

The Mystery：The Nabataeans carved an entire city directly into rose-red sandstone cliffs: the Treasury (Al-Khazneh), the Monastery (Ad-Deir), and other facades tens of metres high, sculpted top-down from solid rock with minimal error. Even more remarkable was the water engineering — in a desert with under 150 mm of annual rainfall, Petra sustained tens of thousands of people through terraces, cisterns, and clay-pipe water systems, hydraulic technology ahead of its time. The Nabataeans left no written account of their engineering; Greek and Roman records are sparse. After the great earthquake of 363 CE the city declined, was forgotten by the Western world from the 12th century, and only "rediscovered" in 1812.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The capital of a Nabataean merchant kingdom, built on spice-trade wealth with masterful rock carving and hydraulic technology; carving proceeded top-down, and the water system was the key to desert survival.

Open Controversy：The precise organisation of the giant facades' construction — scaffolding and surveying techniques — is undocumented; the Treasury's purpose (tomb or temple) is still disputed.

#15 Mada'in Saleh / Hegra 玛达因萨利赫

Location：沙特阿拉伯麦地那省欧拉地区　Date：约公元前 1 世纪–公元 1 世纪（纳巴泰时期）

The Mystery：Hegra, the Nabataean kingdom's second city after Petra, has 111 tombs carved directly into isolated sandstone monoliths, their facades decorated with stepped "crow-step" merlons and griffin reliefs. Most famous is Qasr al-Farid, the "Lonely Castle" — a tomb on a solitary rock, only half-finished, its lower half still rough stone, as if the work were suddenly abandoned. Unlike Petra, most tomb occupants are unidentified, their inscriptions weathered away; whether the "Thamud" mentioned in the Qur'an are connected to this place is endlessly debated; long restricted for religious reasons, the site was only listed as World Heritage in 2008.

Mainstream Archaeological View：An important city and necropolis on the Nabataean caravan route, its tomb styles blending Nabataean, Greco-Roman, and Egyptian elements; the "Thamud" were an earlier Arab tribe, with limited inscriptional evidence.

Open Controversy：Precise dating and the identities of most tomb owners are unclear; the correspondence between religious tradition and archaeological evidence is unresolved.

#16 Osireion 奥西里翁神庙

Location：埃及阿拜多斯（Abydos），塞提一世神庙后方　Date：约公元前 1290 年（第十九王朝塞提一世时期）；部分研究者认为其巨石结构风格迥异，可能年代更早

The Mystery：The Osireion is a half-buried megalithic structure whose core is piled from unadorned blocks of granite and sandstone weighing tens of tonnes each, in a massive, rugged style that contrasts sharply with the finely decorated Temple of Seti I in front of it. It bears no construction inscriptions, and is chronically flooded by groundwater. The puzzle: why would so monumental a stonework go undecorated and break so sharply with New Kingdom style? Water-erosion traces and stratigraphy lead some scholars to suspect it predates Seti I — perhaps an older, unknown building tradition.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Considered a cenotaph Seti I built for the god Osiris, symbolising the god's tomb and forming a ritual whole with Seti's temple; the groundwater problem is attributed to Nile water-table changes.

Open Controversy：The weathering and water-erosion of the blocks, the missing inscriptions, and the stylistic break with surrounding buildings are taken by some as evidence of a pre–New Kingdom date, but direct dating material is lacking and the mainstream still assigns it to Seti I's reign.

#17 Great Zimbabwe 大津巴布韦

Location：津巴布韦东南部　Date：约公元 11–15 世纪（绍纳人祖先所建，鼎盛期约 13–15 世纪）

The Mystery：Great Zimbabwe is the largest ancient stone-built site in sub-Saharan Africa. Its "Great Enclosure" wall stands 11 metres high and runs about 250 metres around, all of granite blocks laid without mortar — and still standing. A 9-metre-high solid conical tower serves no known purpose. In the colonial era, Europeans refused to credit an African civilization, inventing "Queen of Sheba" and "Phoenician" builders; even today the functions of the Great Enclosure and the conical tower are unsettled.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A royal capital and gold-trade centre founded by Shona ancestors; the royal household occupied the Hill Complex, and the Great Enclosure was royal or ritual architecture; the conical tower may have had symbolic or religious meaning.

Open Controversy：The colonial-era "foreign builders" theory has been refuted by archaeology, but details — the specific functions of the enclosures, the tower's exact purpose, the nature of its social organisation — are still under discussion.

#18 Tiwanaku 蒂亚瓦纳科

Location：玻利维亚，的的喀喀湖以南约 20 公里，海拔约 3850 米　Date：约公元 300–1000 年（鼎盛期约 500–900 年）

The Mystery：Tiwanaku was a major centre of pre-Columbian Andean civilization, famed for precise stonework. The Gate of the Sun was carved from a single block of andesite weighing about 10 tonnes, its lintel covered in fine reliefs; the Kalasasaya temple's megaliths fit with tight joints and regular cuts. In the early 20th century the Austrian scholar Arthur Posnansky claimed, from astronomical calculations, that the site was over ten thousand years old — a long-running controversy. How andesite was quarried, transported, and precision-worked at 3,850 metres altitude without metal tools remains a persistent technical question.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The political and religious centre of the Tiwanaku empire; stone was cut and polished with stone hammers, copper tools, and abrasives; radiocarbon dating places the main buildings at 500–900 CE, and Posnansky's ten-thousand-year claim is not accepted.

Open Controversy：The precision of the stone joints and the logistics under contemporary technology still raise questions; the causes of the site's decline (drought, internal unrest) are also unresolved.

#19 Puma Punku 普玛彭古

Location：玻利维亚，蒂亚瓦纳科遗址群的一部分　Date：约公元 536–600 年（主流断代；为蒂亚瓦纳科文化晚期建筑）

The Mystery：Puma Punku is famed for its mass of oddly shaped, precisely cut stones — above all the "H-blocks": andesite and diorite cut into regular right angles, planes, and grooves, fitted seamlessly block to block, with neatly drilled round holes — an overall "machined" look. Critics argue such "industrial-grade" precision was impossible in a Stone-and-Copper-Age world. Yet experimental archaeology has shown that, with abrasives and patience, Stone Age tools can indeed achieve similar results.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Tiwanaku craftsmen worked hard stone by percussion with stone hammers and grinding with sand; the site may look "ruined" because the project was interrupted or later damaged — not because of any mysterious workshop.

Open Controversy：The right-angle precision and standardisation of the blocks is extremely rare among contemporary sites; the source of the "machined" look, why the project was abandoned, and the original form of the finished buildings remain open questions.

#20 Nazca Lines 纳斯卡线

Location：秘鲁南部纳斯卡荒漠　Date：约公元前 500 年–公元 500 年（帕拉卡斯文化晚期至纳斯卡文化时期）

The Mystery：The Nazca desert is etched with hundreds of straight lines, geometric figures, and animal and plant designs hundreds of metres long — nearly invisible from the ground, fully legible only from the air. No written record explains their purpose; Erich von Däniken once proposed "alien runways". Archaeological evidence shows the lines were made by removing dark surface stones to expose the lighter soil beneath — the technique itself is unmysterious. The real puzzle is motive: why make giant pictures meant only to be appreciated from the sky? Astronomical calendars, religious ritual, and groundwater markers are among the competing hypotheses, none conclusive.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual landscape of the Nazca people, used for religious processions and sacrifices; some lines may relate to astronomical observation or water-source marking — the "alien runway" idea has no evidential support.

Open Controversy：Whether the "aerial visibility" was intentional design or coincidence, what the specific rituals were, and the overall planning logic of the line network are all unconfirmed; new figures are still being discovered (with the help of AI and aerial survey).

#21 Moai, Easter Island 复活节岛摩艾石像

Location：智利瓦尔帕莱索大区，复活节岛（拉帕努伊）　Date：约公元 1100–1680 年（雕刻与竖立期）

The Mystery：Nearly 900 moai stand on the island, averaging about 4 metres tall and 12.5 tonnes; the largest, unfinished, reaches about 21 metres and 270 tonnes. Carved at the Rano Raraku quarry, they had to be moved kilometres to coastal ahu platforms and raised — without wheels, lifting machinery, or metal tools. How the islanders transported and erected them — rocked upright in a "walking" motion, or dragged lying down — remains a focus of experimental-archaeology debate. After about 1680 statue-building suddenly stopped and many erected statues were toppled, for reasons equally unknown.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The moai were carved by the Rapa Nui people to honour ancestors and chiefs, facing inland to watch over their descendants; transport and raising were done with ropes, wooden sledges, and massed human labour.

Open Controversy：The manpower and timber the transport required sits uneasily with the island's ecological carrying capacity; the "ecocide" hypothesis (forests felled to move statues, causing civilizational collapse) has been challenged by recent research.

#22 Nan Madol 南马都尔

Location：密克罗尼西亚联邦，波纳佩岛东南泻湖　Date：人工岛始建约公元 1–2 世纪，主体扩建约公元 1180–1628 年（绍德雷尔王朝时期）

The Mystery：Some 100 artificial islets, linked by canals and covering about 80 hectares, carry walls, causeways, and tombs built from hundreds of thousands of tonnes of columnar basalt. Individual basalt columns weigh tonnes and run over 8 metres long; quarried across the island, they were carried to the shallows and stacked "log-cabin" fashion — by builders with no metal tools and no large boats. What kind of social organisation accomplished this, and why build on lagoon shallows rather than land, have no complete answers; some scholars think earlier building layers may lie underwater.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Nan Madol was the political and religious centre of the Saudeleur dynasty, built by a priestly class organising labour to raft basalt columns to the shallows and stack them into artificial islands.

Open Controversy：Whether the required manpower matches Pohnpei's population at the time is questioned; if the suspected underwater structures are real, the construction date could be pushed much earlier.

#23 Yonaguni Monument 与那国岛海底遗迹

Location：日本冲绳县，与那国岛近海海底（水深约 25 米）　Date：未定——若为人工，推测形成于约 8000–10000 年前的末次冰期；若为自然，则为中新世砂岩地层

The Mystery：In 1986 a diver found off this sea a rock formation about 150 metres long, 40 metres wide, and 25 metres high, with terraced steps, straight narrow waterways, arches, and apparent stone pillars — strikingly like an artificial complex. Whether natural sandstone jointing can produce such regular right angles and straight lines has divided geologists and the discoverer for nearly forty years; no confirmed artifacts or tool marks have been found, and the monument's true nature remains undecided.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Most geologists regard it as natural sandstone strata, the terraced look produced by jointing and wave erosion.

Open Controversy：Discoverer Masaaki Kimura insists on the artificial theory, citing wedge-cut marks and apparent carvings; rebuttals hold these features to be natural jointing.

#24 Longyou Caves 龙游石窟

Location：中国浙江省衢州市龙游县　Date：未定，推测为先秦至汉代（约 2000 年前），无文字记载可考

The Mystery：In 1992, after villagers drained a pond, 24 vast man-made underground caverns came to light, each covering up to thousands of square metres and reaching 30 metres deep. Their walls are covered in neat parallel diagonal chisel marks, each about 60 cm long and spaced with remarkable regularity — evidence of a highly organised workforce. Who dug so vast a project, for what purpose, and when — yet official histories and local gazetteers say not a word about it. The contrast between the chisel marks' regularity, the caverns' scale, and this total "silence of the records" is Longyou's greatest unsolved riddle.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Possibly quarries or storage/military installations from the pre-Qin to Han period, the wall marks being construction traces left by level-by-level excavation.

Open Controversy：Why so large a project left no trace in the historical record; whether the chisel marks' consistency exceeds pure handwork; tomb and Daoist-altar theories all lack supporting evidence.

#25 Sanxingdui 三星堆

Location：中国四川省广汉市　Date：约公元前 1300–前 1100 年（商代晚期，距今约 3000–3300 年）

The Mystery：In 1986 two sacrificial pits yielded over a thousand bronzes: a 3.95-metre bronze sacred tree, a 2.62-metre bronze standing figure, and a bronze mask 66 cm wide with protruding eyes — a style wholly unlike the Shang culture of the Central Plains. The ancient Shu kingdom appears in no transmitted literature, only scattered notes such as the Huayang Guozhi. The objects were deliberately burned, smashed, and buried in layers — whether this great "destruction and burial" was a heaven-worship rite or a dynastic-change sacrifice marking a conquest, archaeology has not decided.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Sanxingdui was the capital of the ancient Shu kingdom; its bronzes were distinctive Shu religious and sacrificial objects, the "protruding-eye" imagery perhaps linked to the Shu king Cancong legend; the pits were burial pits after sacrifice.

Open Controversy：With no written Shu records, the history cannot be reconstructed; motives for the burning and burial are disputed; the bronzes' casting-technology origins and stylistic uniqueness are still being studied.
