Archive of Mysterious Ruins

Our stance: The world is scattered with ancient sites that remain genuinely unexplained — how the megaliths were moved, whether astronomical alignments are coincidence or design, what the builders truly intended. This archive admits only sites with real scholarly controversy, presenting both mainstream archaeological interpretations and the open questions side by side, without endorsing supernatural claims. #01 Stonehenge 巨石阵

Location：英国威尔特郡　Date：约公元前 3000–1600 年（分期建造）

The Mystery：Built in multiple phases, Stonehenge's heaviest sarsen stones weigh about 25 tonnes, while its "bluestones" came from the Preseli Hills of Wales, 225 km away. How Late Neolithic people moved these blocks over such distances and raised them in precise mortise-and-tenon joints — without wheeled vehicles or metal tools — remains unresolved. The monument's main axis aligns with the midsummer sunrise; whether it served as an observatory, a burial ground, a healing sanctuary, or a place of ancestor worship has been debated for over a century. A 2024 study further traced the central Altar Stone to northeastern Scotland, some 750 km away, adding a new transport puzzle.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A multi-phase ritual and funerary monument complex; the solstitial alignments served a seasonal calendar and ancestor cult, reflecting the complex social organisation of Late Neolithic society.

Open Controversy：How the bluestones were transported is Stonehenge's longest-running debate. In 1923 geologist Herbert Thomas first traced them to the Preseli Hills of Wales and argued for human transport; UCL archaeologist Mike Parker Pearson further holds the overland route most likely — a team of 60 could move a 2-tonne stone on a wooden lattice. Geomorphologist Brian John, however, has long championed glacial transport in books such as The Bluestone Enigma, claiming the Irish Sea Glacier delivered the stones to Salisbury Plain for people to collect. Petrographic work by Bevins et al. (2025) counters that his key evidence, the Newall boulder, is a human-transported rhyolite fragment, leaving the glacial theory 'lacking evidential support.' On function, astronomer Gerald Hawkins claimed in Stonehenge Decoded (1965) that the monument was a prehistoric astronomical 'computer'; Parker Pearson's Stonehenge Riverside Project instead interprets it as a cremation cemetery and centre of ancestor worship. The two camps remain at odds.

#02 Avebury 阿维伯里

Location：英国威尔特郡　Date：约公元前 2850–2200 年

The Mystery：Avebury is one of Europe's largest Neolithic stone circles, its outer ring more than 300 metres across with nearly a hundred original stones — far larger than Stonehenge, yet long overlooked. Its exact purpose is unknown: an astronomical observatory, a religious ritual centre, or a ground model of a lost cosmology? Medieval villagers toppled and buried many of the stones, leaving the original layout incomplete. Together with nearby Silbury Hill and West Kennet Long Barrow it forms a vast ritual landscape that suggests a unified plan — but the planners' intent can no longer be recovered.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The core of a Neolithic ritual landscape, part of a long-used ceremonial and funerary complex together with the surrounding barrows and mounds, probably linked to ancestor worship and seasonal rites.

Open Controversy：The circle's exact function remains undecided. The 18th-century antiquary William Stukeley claimed it was the work of druids — a view archaeology has discarded; the modern mainstream sees a Neolithic ritual gathering place, though the specific rites are unknown. The monument is itself a history of destruction: in the 14th century locals toppled and buried many stones for religious reasons — excavator Alexander Keiller uncovered the skeleton of a 'barber surgeon' crushed by a falling stone in the 1930s — and more stone was carted off for building in the 17th–18th centuries. Half of what visitors see today was re-erected by Keiller, the marmalade-heir archaeologist, in 1937–1939. Without politician-archaeologist John Lubbock (later Baron Avebury) buying up the land in the 1870s to halt village expansion, even less might survive.

#03 Newgrange 纽格莱奇墓

Location：爱尔兰米斯郡（博因谷）　Date：约公元前 3200 年

The Mystery：This roughly 5,200-year-old passage tomb predates Stonehenge by about a thousand years and the Great Pyramid of Giza by about five hundred. Each winter solstice, a beam of sunlight passes through the "roof-box" above the entrance and travels 19 metres down the passage into the chamber's depths for about 17 minutes — whether this precise alignment was deliberate design or coincidence is still disputed. The entrance stone and chamber slabs are carved with spirals, triskele and other megalithic art whose symbolism is undeciphered. The white quartz façade, rebuilt in the 1970s, has also long been questioned for its fidelity to the original.

Mainstream Archaeological View：An elite burial and ritual monument of a Neolithic farming society, the solstice illumination linked to ancestor worship and seasonal rites.

Open Controversy：The white quartz facade visitors see today was rebuilt in 1967–1974 by the excavator, UCC archaeologist Michael J. O'Kelly, following his own interpretation — with a modern concrete support wall hidden behind it. French archaeologist P. R. Giot mocked it as looking like 'a cream cheese cake with dried currants'; archaeologist Geraldine Stout and historical geographer Matthew Stout argued in a 2008 book that the wall rests on O'Kelly's personal reading rather than firm evidence; Danish archaeologist Palle Eriksen goes further, holding there was no Neolithic wall at all and the quartz originally lay flat before the entrance. In 2016 archaeologist Michael Gibbons called the roof-box a 1960s fabrication, but four senior archaeologists, including the National Museum's Keeper of Irish Antiquities, rebutted him in The Irish Times, noting the roof-box had been photographed since at least the 1930s. The spiral motifs still have no generally accepted interpretation.

#04 Carnac stones 卡纳克巨石阵

Location：法国布列塔尼　Date：约公元前 4600–3300 年（2025 年发表于《Antiquity》的最新测年显示，部分石列可早至公元前 4600–4300 年，为欧洲最早的巨石建筑之一）

The Mystery：Stretching some 10 kilometres across the Carnac region, more than 3,000 standing stones stand in over a dozen rows — the largest prehistoric stone-row complex in the world, more than a millennium older than Stonehenge. Why expend such enormous labour to line thousands of stones in straight rows? Astronomical sight-lines, pilgrimage procession ways, burial boundaries, or a ground-level expression of a lost cosmic order? The builders left no writing, so every explanation rests on indirect evidence; the rows' true purpose remains a mystery.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual landscape built up over a long Neolithic period, possibly connected with ancestor worship, territorial marking, or communal ceremony.

Open Controversy：The rows' purpose is much disputed. In 1887 French scholar H. de Cleuziou argued they align with solstitial sunsets; in 1970–1974 Oxford engineer Alexander Thom and his son Archie Thom surveyed the alignments in detail, proposing astronomical alignments in support of Thom's 'megalithic yard' hypothesis — itself challenged by statisticians. As for the popular 'seismic detector' idea, i.e. that the rows were a Neolithic earthquake-sensing device, no named scholarly proponent can be identified; it circulates mainly as internet lore and lacks evidential support.

#05 Callanish Stones 卡拉尼什巨石阵

Location：苏格兰刘易斯岛　Date：约公元前 2900–2600 年（新石器时代晚期）

The Mystery：The Callanish stones on the Isle of Lewis form a rare cruciform plan: a central monolith 4.8 metres tall ringed by 13 stones, with four rows of stones radiating outward. Organising such an enterprise on a remote island is itself a puzzle of social organisation. Most controversial is the "astronomical alignment" claim: the rows are said to point to the extreme rising and setting positions of the Moon at the "major lunar standstill" of its 18.6-year cycle, making Callanish a prehistoric lunar observatory — but critics argue the alignments may be cherry-picked coincidences, and the monument's true function remains unexplained.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual centre in long use from the Late Neolithic into the Bronze Age; the cruciform plan may echo celestial movements or a cosmology, and burials within the circle point to a funerary role.

Open Controversy：The lunar-alignment theory was put forward by Oxford engineering professor Alexander Thom. In 1971 he coined the term 'lunar standstill', arguing that Neolithic circle builders understood the Moon's 18.6-year nodal cycle and marked its southernmost risings with stones; astronomer Gerald Hawkins likewise backed a 'prehistoric lunar observatory'. Researchers Ron and Margaret Curtis have spent decades verifying the Callanish alignments. Critics reply that any stone setting will yield some chance alignments, and with millennia of weathering and displacement, original design intent can never be confirmed. The specific rituals performed there are unrecoverable.

#06 Maltese Megalithic Temples 马耳他巨石神殿

Location：马耳他（Ġgantija 神殿位于戈佐岛，Ħaġar Qim 神殿位于马耳他主岛西南海岸）　Date：约公元前 3600–2500 年（Ġgantija 神殿约建于公元前 3600 年，早于埃及金字塔与英国巨石阵）

The Mystery：The Maltese islands hold seven megalithic temple sites (a World Heritage listing), built of stones weighing tens of tonnes. How the builders quarried, transported, and raised these blocks without metal tools or the wheel has never been fully reconstructed. Ħaġar Qim shows precise solar alignments: at the summer and winter solstices, sunlight passes through a specific doorway into the inner apse. Stranger still, the "temple builder" culture ended abruptly around 2500 BCE, followed by centuries with almost no human occupation — for reasons unknown.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Religious ritual centres of Neolithic farming communities, built with stone-ball rollers, levers, ramps, and massed human labour; the culture's interruption may relate to drought, soil degradation, or social upheaval.

Open Controversy：The temple culture broke off around 2500 BCE for reasons still debated. Archaeological evidence shows soil erosion, deforestation and signs of malnutrition; ice cores record a climatic downturn around 2350 BCE, and a UCL doctoral study blames demographic pressure compounded by drought. Notably, there is no evidence of invasion or plague. As for the claim that the blocks' scale and precision exceed Neolithic technical limits, no named scholarly proponent can be identified — it circulates mainly online; the official position of UNESCO and Malta's Superintendence of Cultural Heritage is that settled farming communities built the temples with flint tools, in a coherent radiocarbon sequence requiring no outside explanation.

#07 Skara Brae 斯卡拉布雷

Location：英国苏格兰奥克尼群岛梅恩兰岛　Date：约公元前 3100–2500 年（新石器时代）

The Mystery：Europe's best-preserved Neolithic village, known as "the Scottish Pompeii". In 1850 a great storm stripped away the covering sand dunes and revealed eight stone houses. Their furnishings are astonishingly intact: stone beds, dressers, and stools, plus a drainage system and what appear to be indoor toilets — a far more sophisticated way of life than the popular image of "primitive man". Around 2500 BCE the inhabitants suddenly left, for reasons unknown; the settlement's regular layout and mature stonework hardly look like beginners' work.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A farming-and-fishing community settlement, built from locally cleaved stone slabs; abandonment may relate to cooling climate, sandstorm encroachment, or changes in subsistence.

Open Controversy：The village was abandoned around 2500 BCE for reasons still debated. Its first systematic excavator was Australian archaeologist V. Gordon Childe, who directed work from 1927 and dubbed it 'Scotland's Pompeii'. A popular story says a great storm buried the village in sand overnight; but archaeologist Anna Ritchie's view — shared by most archaeologists today — is that abandonment was gradual: wind-blown sand buried the houses over generations while residents drifted to Bronze Age farmsteads. The great storm of 1850 is real, but it stripped the dunes to reveal the site rather than causing its abandonment. Whether the village's uniform planning implies organisation above tribal level is still discussed.

#08 Teotihuacan 特奥蒂瓦坎

Location：墨西哥墨西哥州，今墨西哥城东北约 40 公里　Date：约公元前 100 年–公元 750 年（鼎盛期约公元 450 年，人口达 10 万–20 万）

The Mystery：One of the largest ancient cities of the Americas, Teotihuacan's Pyramids of the Sun and Moon and its "Avenue of the Dead" form a strict gridded city plan aligned to astronomical directions. Yet which people built it remains unanswered — the Aztecs found it already in ruins and called it "the place where the gods were born"; they were not its builders. Around 750 CE the temples and palaces of the city centre were systematically burned — invasion or internal revolt, the evidence is inconclusive. Liquid mercury found in a recently discovered tunnel beneath the Pyramid of the Moon is equally unexplained.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A multi-ethnic city-state, the religious and trading hub of Mesoamerica; its decline is linked to 6th–8th-century droughts, elite infighting, and the defection of subordinate cities.

Open Controversy：The builders' ethnic identity remains unresolved; scholars lean toward a multi-ethnic city rather than a single people. In 2015, INAH archaeologist Sergio Gómez Chávez announced that large quantities of liquid mercury — never before seen in Mesoamerican archaeology — had been found at the end of a tunnel sealed 1,800 years earlier beneath the Feathered Serpent Pyramid. Gómez holds the mercury symbolised the rivers and lakes of the underworld, making the tunnel a crafted model of the afterlife; other archaeologists suggest it may herald Teotihuacan's first ruler's tomb. Around 550 CE the ceremonial centre was systematically burned — invasion or internal uprising is still undecided.

#09 Tikal 蒂卡尔

Location：危地马拉佩滕省热带雨林　Date：约公元前 400 年–公元 900 年（古典期玛雅城邦，鼎盛于 8 世纪）

The Mystery：One of the most powerful Classic Maya city-states, Tikal's Great Plaza is ringed by stepped pyramids including the 47-metre-tall Temple I. In the 9th century Tikal, along with the whole Classic Maya civilization, fell into the "Great Collapse"; the city was abandoned and swallowed by jungle for a millennium, until its rediscovery in the 19th century. Tikal waged a century-long hegemonic war with Calakmul 130 km away, with fortunes reversing repeatedly; in 378 CE a mysterious "arrival of strangers", possibly linked to Teotihuacan, rewrote Tikal's dynastic sequence. Structures such as the E-Group show precise solstitial alignments.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A political and religious centre of a Maya city-state, with 60,000–90,000 inhabitants at its peak; the abandonment resulted from a combination of 9th-century drought, chronic warfare, overpopulation, and political breakdown.

Open Controversy：Whether the 'Collapse' had a single cause or many has been debated for decades. Geographer Jared Diamond argued in Collapse (2005) for ecological overshoot causing drought; Richardson Gill used climatic evidence in The Great Maya Droughts (2000) to make drought the prime cause; archaeologist Arthur Demarest stresses warfare and political breakdown; David Webster, in The Fall of the Ancient Maya, holds that kings' failure to uphold order through ritual collapsed the kingship system. Most researchers now grant drought 'the final nail in the coffin' while accepting multiple causes. The 378 CE 'arrival' — the Teotihuacan figure Siyaj K'ak' reaching Tikal, his name deciphered by epigrapher David Stuart — remains undecided as military expedition or dynastic marriage.

#10 Sacsayhuamán 萨克塞华曼

Location：秘鲁库斯科城北山丘　Date：约 15 世纪（印加帝国时期，图帕克·印加·尤潘基统治时期始建）

The Mystery：The Inca fortress above Cusco: three zigzagging walls built of polygonal blocks weighing over a hundred tonnes, fitted so tightly that a knife blade cannot pass between them — all without mortar. The Inca had no wheels, no iron tools, and no large draft animals; how these blocks were quarried kilometres away, transported, cut into irregular polygons, and fitted seamlessly remains a classic engineering puzzle, with the zigzag plan itself suspected of anti-seismic design. In 1536 it was the main Inca stronghold during the siege of the Spanish.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A military and religious fortress built by the Inca empire with tens of thousands of labourers using stone and bronze tools, ropes, ramps, levers, and human power; modern reconstruction experiments show that trial-fitting and grinding can achieve seamless joints.

Open Controversy：The claim that the workmanship exceeds Inca tooling is popular online, but no credentialed archaeologist is on record supporting it. In 1985 UC Berkeley architectural historian Jean-Pierre Protzen reproduced Inca stone dressing and fitting with nothing but river-cobble hammerstones, showing trial-and-error fitting achieves the tight joints — 'no two blocks share the same angles, which is what you get when there is no standard cut.' Spanish chronicler Pedro Cieza de León recorded in 1553 the Inca's own account: some twenty thousand men in rotation, four thousand quarrying, six thousand hauling with leather-and-fibre cables. In 1609 chronicler Garcilaso de la Vega marvelled that it seemed 'made by enchantment, the work of demons and not of men' — though his book's very purpose was to defend the Inca. The blocks were quarried on the same hill as the fortress, not far away.

#11 Baalbek 巴勒贝克

Location：黎巴嫩贝卡谷地　Date：罗马时期（朱庇特神庙约公元 1 世纪）；巨石基座年代有争议

The Mystery：The foundations of Baalbek's Temple of Jupiter carry three enormous blocks — the Trilithon — each about 19–20 metres long and weighing an estimated 800–1,000 tonnes, among the largest stones ever moved by human hands. Nearby quarries hold even bigger ones: the "Stone of the Pregnant Woman" (about 1,000 tonnes) and an unnamed block estimated at 1,650 tonnes. Moving, transporting, and setting thousand-tonne blocks with Roman-era lifting machines and levers has never been replicated; and the megalithic foundation's masonry style differs markedly from the Roman stonework above, leading some to suspect the base belongs to an earlier, unknown building phase.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The Temple of Jupiter is a Roman imperial expansion of an earlier Phoenician/Greek sanctuary; the giant blocks demonstrate Roman engineering, moved by human labour, levers, rollers, and earthen ramps.

Open Controversy：French archaeologist Jean-Pierre Adam's 1977 study calculated that the Romans, with compound pulleys, capstans and imperial military logistics, were fully capable of moving the Trilithon blocks — the "Romans couldn't have done it" claim doesn't hold; yet thousand-tonne transport has never been replicated in modern times. As for whether the temple platform predates Rome: the stylistic break is real, but the "pre-Roman platform" case is made mainly by alternative-history writers (e.g., British writer Graham Hancock), with no named archaeologist backing it from excavation evidence — the date remains undetermined.

#12 Göbekli Tepe 哥贝克力石阵

Location：土耳其东南部尚勒乌尔法附近　Date：约公元前 9600–前 8200 年（前陶新石器时代）

The Mystery：Around 12,000 years ago, before farming, a group of hunter-gatherers raised on a stone hill a complex of circular enclosures ringed by dozens of T-shaped pillars: multi-tonne stones carved with foxes, snakes, vultures, scorpions, and other animals. This predates writing, the wheel, and metal by millennia, and the pyramids by about 7,000 years. It upends the traditional sequence of "agriculture first, then complex society, then religious architecture" — what social organisation let hunter-gatherers sustain such quarrying, transport, and carving? Around 8000 BCE the site was deliberately backfilled, for motives still unknown.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual site built by pre-pottery Neolithic hunter-gatherer communities, proving complex ritual and cooperation could precede agriculture — "belief before farming".

Open Controversy：Klaus Schmidt of the German Archaeological Institute, who directed excavations from 1995 to 2014, argued "first the temple, then the city" — ritual demand created complex society, i.e. belief before agriculture — and that the enclosures were deliberately backfilled. His successor Lee Clare (DAI Istanbul) countered with new evidence: a 2020 study attributed the fill to natural slope-slide deposits, and domestic houses, hearths and a rock-cut cistern were found — not a purely ceremonial site. British writer Graham Hancock's Magicians of the Gods (2015) claims the site as proof of a lost Ice Age civilization, rejected by the excavation team. How the fine carving was achieved with Stone Age tools remains open.

#13 Derinkuyu 德林库尤地下城

Location：土耳其卡帕多西亚内夫谢希尔省　Date：约公元前 8–7 世纪始建（弗里几亚时期），拜占庭时期大规模扩建

The Mystery：Beneath the Cappadocian tuff lies a vertical city some 60 metres deep with 18 levels, able to house tens of thousands of people and livestock, complete with ventilation shafts, granaries, wineries, schools, churches, tombs, and massive stone doors that could be sealed from inside. Hardest to explain is the ventilation system: dozens of shafts piercing tens of metres of rock to carry air and water to the depths — an astonishing feat of precision and scale under ancient technical conditions. Tunnels run for kilometres to the neighbouring underground city of Kaymakli. Who dug it, when, and with what tools has no certain answer.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A refuge city begun in Phrygian times and repeatedly expanded by Byzantine Christians to escape war and persecution; the shafts and tunnels were designed for long-term shelter.

Open Controversy：Turkey's Ministry of Culture attributes the earliest galleries to the Phrygians (8th–7th c. BCE), massively expanded as a refuge city in the Byzantine era; the Athenian writer Xenophon described underground dwellings in Anatolia in his Anabasis (c. 370 BCE) as a parallel. As for "earlier origins" (Hittite, or even a 12,000-year-old Younger Dryas refuge): no named archaeologist supports these with evidence — they remain internet folklore. How the inter-city tunnels were organized and the ventilation shafts engineered is still an open question in engineering history.

#14 Petra 佩特拉

Location：约旦南部马安省　Date：纳巴泰王国鼎盛期，约公元前 1 世纪–公元 1 世纪

The Mystery：The Nabataeans carved an entire city directly into rose-red sandstone cliffs: the Treasury (Al-Khazneh), the Monastery (Ad-Deir), and other facades tens of metres high, sculpted top-down from solid rock with minimal error. Even more remarkable was the water engineering — in a desert with under 150 mm of annual rainfall, Petra sustained tens of thousands of people through terraces, cisterns, and clay-pipe water systems, hydraulic technology ahead of its time. The Nabataeans left no written account of their engineering; Greek and Roman records are sparse. After the great earthquake of 363 CE the city declined, was forgotten by the Western world from the 12th century, and only "rediscovered" in 1812.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The capital of a Nabataean merchant kingdom, built on spice-trade wealth with masterful rock carving and hydraulic technology; carving proceeded top-down, and the water system was the key to desert survival.

Open Controversy：The mainstream identification of Al-Khazneh is a royal tomb: after the 2024 tomb discovery, Tim Kinnaird of the University of St Andrews stated it was "most likely built as a mausoleum and crypt in the early 1st century AD for Aretas IV (r. 9 BCE–40 CE)". The temple hypothesis appears in popular accounts but has no named representative scholar behind it. The top-down construction sequence, scaffolding and surveying of the giant facades are entirely undocumented and can only be inferred.

#15 Mada'in Saleh / Hegra 玛达因萨利赫

Location：沙特阿拉伯麦地那省欧拉地区　Date：约公元前 1 世纪–公元 1 世纪（纳巴泰时期）

The Mystery：Hegra, the Nabataean kingdom's second city after Petra, has 111 tombs carved directly into isolated sandstone monoliths, their facades decorated with stepped "crow-step" merlons and griffin reliefs. Most famous is Qasr al-Farid, the "Lonely Castle" — a tomb on a solitary rock, only half-finished, its lower half still rough stone, as if the work were suddenly abandoned. Unlike Petra, most tomb occupants are unidentified, their inscriptions weathered away; whether the "Thamud" mentioned in the Qur'an are connected to this place is endlessly debated; long restricted for religious reasons, the site was only listed as World Heritage in 2008.

Mainstream Archaeological View：An important city and necropolis on the Nabataean caravan route, its tomb styles blending Nabataean, Greco-Roman, and Egyptian elements; the "Thamud" were an earlier Arab tribe, with limited inscriptional evidence.

Open Controversy：Laila Nehmé of the French CNRS, directing the Saudi-French excavations since 2002, confirmed that 30 of the 111 monumental tombs bear Nabataean inscriptions (earliest 1 BCE/1 CE, latest 75 CE), belonging to Nabataean elites and peaking under Aretas IV. Islamic tradition identifies the site with the Qur'anic city of Thamud, but the Thamudic and Lihyanite inscriptions found there belong to earlier, different traditions than the tomb builders — the match between religious narrative and archaeology still needs clarification. Precise dates and owners of the uninscribed tombs remain unknown.

#16 Osireion 奥西里翁神庙

Location：埃及阿拜多斯（Abydos），塞提一世神庙后方　Date：约公元前 1290 年（第十九王朝塞提一世时期）

The Mystery：The Osireion is a half-buried megalithic structure whose core is piled from unadorned blocks of granite and sandstone weighing tens of tonnes each, in a massive, rugged style that contrasts sharply with the finely decorated Temple of Seti I in front of it. It bears no construction inscriptions, and is chronically flooded by groundwater. The puzzle: why would so monumental a stonework go undecorated and break so sharply with New Kingdom style? Water-erosion traces and stratigraphy lead some scholars to suspect it predates Seti I — perhaps an older, unknown building tradition.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Considered a cenotaph Seti I built for the god Osiris, symbolising the god's tomb and forming a ritual whole with Seti's temple; the groundwater problem is attributed to Nile water-table changes.

Open Controversy：Both sides of the dating debate have names. Swiss Egyptologist Edouard Naville (excavated 1912–1914) and British archaeologist Margaret Murray held it predates Seti I, even predynastic Egypt; British writer Graham Hancock asserts in Fingerprints of the Gods that it is Egypt's oldest building and Seti I merely restored it. On the other side, Dutch archaeologist Henri Frankfort (excavated 1925–1926 for the Egypt Exploration Society) attributed it to Seti I in his 1933 report on the basis of Seti's cartouches; Egyptologist P.J. Brand's 2000 dissertation re-confirmed the dating epigraphically. The mainstream currently sides with Frankfort, but direct dating evidence is still lacking.

#17 Great Zimbabwe 大津巴布韦

Location：津巴布韦东南部　Date：约公元 11–15 世纪（绍纳人祖先所建，鼎盛期约 13–15 世纪）

The Mystery：Great Zimbabwe is the largest ancient stone-built site in sub-Saharan Africa. Its "Great Enclosure" wall stands 11 metres high and runs about 250 metres around, all of granite blocks laid without mortar — and still standing. A 9-metre-high solid conical tower serves no known purpose. In the colonial era, Europeans refused to credit an African civilization, inventing "Queen of Sheba" and "Phoenician" builders; even today the functions of the Great Enclosure and the conical tower are unsettled.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A royal capital and gold-trade centre founded by Shona ancestors; the royal household occupied the Hill Complex, and the Great Enclosure was royal or ritual architecture; the conical tower may have had symbolic or religious meaning.

Open Controversy：The "foreign builders" story starts with German explorer Karl Mauch, who in 1871 declared it the palace of the Queen of Sheba, biblical Ophir. It was overturned by two generations of British archaeologists: David Randall-MacIver, who after his 1905 excavation first asserted its African origin and medieval date; and Gertrude Caton-Thompson, whose 1929–1931 excavations concluded in The Zimbabwe Culture (1931) that not a single item of evidence contradicted Bantu origin. Archaeologist Peter Garlake's Great Zimbabwe (1973) gave the modern synthesis. Still unresolved: the specific functions of the Great Enclosure's zones, the Conical Tower's exact purpose (granary? symbol of kingship?), and why the city was abandoned around 1450.

#18 Tiwanaku 蒂亚瓦纳科

Location：玻利维亚，的的喀喀湖以南约 20 公里，海拔约 3850 米　Date：约公元 300–1000 年（鼎盛期约 500–900 年）

The Mystery：Tiwanaku was a major centre of pre-Columbian Andean civilization, famed for precise stonework. The Gate of the Sun was carved from a single block of andesite weighing about 10 tonnes, its lintel covered in fine reliefs; the Kalasasaya temple's megaliths fit with tight joints and regular cuts. In the early 20th century the Austrian scholar Arthur Posnansky claimed, from astronomical calculations, that the site was over ten thousand years old — a long-running controversy. How andesite was quarried, transported, and precision-worked at 3,850 metres altitude without metal tools remains a persistent technical question.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The political and religious centre of the Tiwanaku empire; stone was cut and polished with stone hammers, copper tools, and abrasives; radiocarbon dating places the main buildings at 500–900 CE, and Posnansky's ten-thousand-year claim is not accepted.

Open Controversy：On the collapse: University of Chicago anthropologist Alan Kolata and Charles Ortloff argued in the 1990s from Quelccaya ice cores and lake sediments for a drought collapse — prolonged aridity from ~AD 1150 destroyed the raised-field agriculture. Clark Erickson (University of Pennsylvania) countered in Antiquity (1999), calling it "neo-environmental determinism"; a 2023 PLOS ONE Bayesian chronology (Marsh et al.) found the social collapse came generations before the drought — no direct link. On stonework precision: even the Spanish chronicler Pedro Cieza de León, visiting in 1549, marveled at not knowing "with what instruments" the stones were worked; modern experimental archaeology shows hammerstones plus abrasives can do it, but the logistics of the time remain puzzling.

#19 Puma Punku 普玛彭古

Location：玻利维亚，蒂亚瓦纳科遗址群的一部分　Date：约公元 536–600 年（主流断代；为蒂亚瓦纳科文化晚期建筑）

The Mystery：Puma Punku is famed for its mass of oddly shaped, precisely cut stones — above all the "H-blocks": andesite and diorite cut into regular right angles, planes, and grooves, fitted seamlessly block to block, with neatly drilled round holes — an overall "machined" look. Critics argue such "industrial-grade" precision was impossible in a Stone-and-Copper-Age world. Yet experimental archaeology has shown that, with abrasives and patience, Stone Age tools can indeed achieve similar results.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Tiwanaku craftsmen worked hard stone by percussion with stone hammers and grinding with sand; the site may look "ruined" because the project was interrupted or later damaged — not because of any mysterious workshop.

Open Controversy：The "machined look" is the ancient-astronaut camp's favorite exhibit — Swiss writer and pseudoarchaeology figurehead Erich von Däniken used it in Chariots of the Gods? (1968) as evidence of extraterrestrial intervention. Archaeologists answer with names too: UC Berkeley's Jean-Pierre Protzen and UCLA's Stella Nair reconstructed the hammerstone-plus-quartz-sand-abrasion chaîne opératoire in The Stones of Tiahuanaco (2013); MIT materials scientist Heather Lechtman documented the copper-arsenic-nickel bronze cramps in 1998; Alexei Vranich 3D-scanned every block and 3D-printed a reconstruction in 2018, proving they belong to one precise design. Why the project was abandoned and the original finished form remain incompletely resolved.

#20 Nazca Lines 纳斯卡线

Location：秘鲁南部纳斯卡荒漠　Date：约公元前 500 年–公元 500 年（帕拉卡斯文化晚期至纳斯卡文化时期）

The Mystery：The Nazca desert is etched with hundreds of straight lines, geometric figures, and animal and plant designs hundreds of metres long — nearly invisible from the ground, fully legible only from the air. No written record explains their purpose; Erich von Däniken once proposed "alien runways". Archaeological evidence shows the lines were made by removing dark surface stones to expose the lighter soil beneath — the technique itself is unmysterious. The real puzzle is motive: why make giant pictures meant only to be appreciated from the sky? Astronomical calendars, religious ritual, and groundwater markers are among the competing hypotheses, none conclusive.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A ritual landscape of the Nazca people, used for religious processions and sacrifices; some lines may relate to astronomical observation or water-source marking — the "alien runway" idea has no evidential support.

Open Controversy：The most crowded cast. American historian Paul Kosok, flying over the desert in 1939, called it "the largest astronomy book in the world"; German mathematician Maria Reiche ("the Lady of the Lines," 40 years on site) developed the astronomical-calendar theory, but American astronomer Gerald Hawkins' 1968 computer comparison of star positions ruled out systematic alignment. Swiss writer and pseudoarchaeology figurehead Erich von Däniken claimed in Chariots of the Gods? (1968) that the lines were alien spacecraft runways — dismissed by scholars as science fiction. American anthropologist Johan Reinhard (National Geographic Explorer-in-Residence) proposed the water-ritual theory: lines oriented toward sacred Andean peaks and water sources, a landscape-scale rainmaking ritual in the hyper-arid desert. Whether aerial visibility was intentional design or coincidence remains unconfirmed.

#21 Moai, Easter Island 复活节岛摩艾石像

Location：智利瓦尔帕莱索大区，复活节岛（拉帕努伊）　Date：约公元 1100–1680 年（雕刻与竖立期）

The Mystery：Nearly 900 moai stand on the island, averaging about 4 metres tall and 12.5 tonnes; the largest, unfinished, reaches about 21 metres and 270 tonnes. Carved at the Rano Raraku quarry, they had to be moved kilometres to coastal ahu platforms and raised — without wheels, lifting machinery, or metal tools. How the islanders transported and erected them — rocked upright in a "walking" motion, or dragged lying down — remains a focus of experimental-archaeology debate. After about 1680 statue-building suddenly stopped and many erected statues were toppled, for reasons equally unknown. Recent discoveries have revealed that some of the statues on Easter Island have their bodies buried underground, with patterned markings on them — only a small portion is visible above ground.

Mainstream Archaeological View：The moai were carved by the Rapa Nui people to honour ancestors and chiefs, facing inland to watch over their descendants; transport and raising were done with ropes, wooden sledges, and massed human labour.

Open Controversy：UCLA geographer Jared Diamond argued in Collapse (2005) for an 'ecocide' hypothesis: islanders felled the forests to move moai, collapsing their civilization. But University of Hawaii archaeologist Terry Hunt and Binghamton University anthropologist Carl Lipo counter that Polynesian rats arriving with the settlers — eating palm seeds — were the main driver of deforestation, not statue-building; a 2024 Nature ancient-DNA study of Rapa Nui found no pre-contact population crash.

#22 Nan Madol 南马都尔

Location：密克罗尼西亚联邦，波纳佩岛东南泻湖　Date：人工岛始建约公元 1–2 世纪，主体扩建约公元 1180–1628 年（绍德雷尔王朝时期）

The Mystery：Some 100 artificial islets, linked by canals and covering about 80 hectares, carry walls, causeways, and tombs built from hundreds of thousands of tonnes of columnar basalt. Individual basalt columns weigh tonnes and run over 8 metres long; quarried across the island, they were carried to the shallows and stacked "log-cabin" fashion — by builders with no metal tools and no large boats. What kind of social organisation accomplished this, and why build on lagoon shallows rather than land, have no complete answers; some scholars think earlier building layers may lie underwater.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Nan Madol was the political and religious centre of the Saudeleur dynasty, built by a priestly class organising labour to raft basalt columns to the shallows and stack them into artificial islands.

Open Controversy：In 1978–79 archaeologist Arthur Saxe's team surveyed the harbor off Nan Madol and reported coral-covered stone pillars up to 6 m tall; but a 1988–89 University of Oregon re-survey found these 'pillars' were natural coral growth, not architecture, and Ishimura et al.'s 2013 multibeam-sonar and ROV survey likewise found no man-made traces. Meanwhile alternative-history writer David Childress (Ancient Aliens) claims a far older sunken city built with extraterrestrial help levitating magnetic basalt, and James Churchward in The Lost Continent of Mu (1926) cast Nan Madol as a relic of sunken Mu — none of which has archaeological support.

#23 Yonaguni Monument 与那国岛海底遗迹

Location：日本冲绳县，与那国岛近海海底（水深约 25 米）　Date：未定——若为人工，推测形成于约 8000–10000 年前的末次冰期；若为自然，则为中新世砂岩地层

The Mystery：In 1986 a diver found off this sea a rock formation about 150 metres long, 40 metres wide, and 25 metres high, with terraced steps, straight narrow waterways, arches, and apparent stone pillars — strikingly like an artificial complex. Whether natural sandstone jointing can produce such regular right angles and straight lines has divided geologists and the discoverer for nearly forty years; no confirmed artifacts or tool marks have been found, and the monument's true nature remains undecided.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Most geologists regard it as natural sandstone strata, the terraced look produced by jointing and wave erosion.

Open Controversy：Discoverer Masaaki Kimura, a geologist at the University of the Ryukyus, insists the structures are artificial, citing quarry marks, stone tools and a tablet with 'writing'; Boston University geologist Robert Schoch, after repeated dives, concludes they are natural — Miocene sandstone whose horizontal bedding planes and vertical joints erode into steps and right angles, with Kimura's 'writing' being natural scratches. German geologist Wolf Wichmann, who studied the site in 1999, likewise supports a natural origin.

#24 Longyou Caves 龙游石窟

Location：中国浙江省衢州市龙游县　Date：未定，推测为先秦至汉代（约 2000 年前），无文字记载可考

The Mystery：In 1992, after villagers drained a pond, 24 vast man-made underground caverns came to light, each covering up to thousands of square metres and reaching 30 metres deep. Their walls are covered in neat parallel diagonal chisel marks, each about 60 cm long and spaced with remarkable regularity — evidence of a highly organised workforce. Who dug so vast a project, for what purpose, and when — yet official histories and local gazetteers say not a word about it. The contrast between the chisel marks' regularity, the caverns' scale, and this total "silence of the records" is Longyou's greatest unsolved riddle.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Possibly quarries or storage/military installations from the pre-Qin to Han period, the wall marks being construction traces left by level-by-level excavation.

Open Controversy：After a dozen field surveys, Zhejiang University's Dr. Chu Liangcai proposed a 'granary' theory: the caves were dug under Emperor Xuan of Han's 'build granaries in border commanderies' policy to store grain as ever-normal granaries; CASS Institute of Archaeology researcher Yang Hongxun instead proposed a 'hidden troops' theory — King Goujian of Yue dug them to secretly train soldiers for his restoration. Both face hard problems: the caves lie below river level and flood, unfit for grain; with a single entrance and poor ventilation, unfit for troops. The tomb theory fails for lack of any remains or grave goods, and the quarry theory cannot explain the walls' regular decorative chisel marks — the purpose remains undetermined.

#25 Sanxingdui 三星堆

Location：中国四川省广汉市　Date：约公元前 1300–前 1100 年（商代晚期，距今约 3000–3300 年）

The Mystery：In 1986 two sacrificial pits yielded over a thousand bronzes: a 3.95-metre bronze sacred tree, a 2.62-metre bronze standing figure, and a bronze mask 66 cm wide with protruding eyes — a style wholly unlike the Shang culture of the Central Plains. The ancient Shu kingdom appears in no transmitted literature, only scattered notes such as the Huayang Guozhi. The objects were deliberately burned, smashed, and buried in layers — whether this great "destruction and burial" was a heaven-worship rite or a dynastic-change sacrifice marking a conquest, archaeology has not decided.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Sanxingdui was the capital of the ancient Shu kingdom; its bronzes were distinctive Shu religious and sacrificial objects, the "protruding-eye" imagery perhaps linked to the Shu king Cancong legend; the pits were burial pits after sacrifice.

Open Controversy：In 2026, a Sichuan Provincial Cultural Relics and Archaeology Research Institute team, comparing pit shapes and burial features, proposed a new view: the large pits' artifacts were not deliberately smashed for sacrifice but buried after a temple fire collapsed and damaged them — ash tested as burned outside the pit and dumped in, with no scorching on pit walls. The long-standing mainstream view, however, held 'destruction sacrifice': sacred objects intentionally broken and buried, the burial pattern recorded by 1986 excavation leader Chen De'an and colleagues. Whether the motive was ritual, 'sealing' before a capital move, or war damage is still debated.

#26 Inca Stone Walls of Cusco 印加石墙（库斯科）

Location：秘鲁库斯科（哈通·鲁米约克街 Hatun Rumiyoc、科里坎查周边）　Date：约 15 世纪（印加帝国时期；部分墙体可能更早）

The Mystery：Cusco's old town still preserves long stretches of Inca stonework: polygonal blocks of varying shapes fitted together so tightly without any mortar that, as legend has it, not even a knife blade can slip into the joints. The most famous is the twelve-angled stone on Hatun Rumiyoc street — a single block cut with twelve angles, interlocking seamlessly with its neighbors. The Inca had no iron tools and no wheels; how they cut hard stone so precisely with stone hammers, bronze tools and manpower, then transported and fitted the blocks, has never been fully reconstructed. The same mortarless megalithic tradition also appears at Sacsayhuaman and Ollantaytambo, and its technological origin remains a mystery.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Inca masons worked stone against stone: harder hammerstones were used to peck and shape each block through repeated trial fitting, backed by massive labor and generations of accumulated skill. Polygonal mortarless masonry also performs exceptionally well in earthquakes, proving more stable in the Andean seismic zone than regular coursed masonry.

Open Controversy：Peruvian archaeologist Carlos Rosell excavated a pre-Inca wall beneath Triunfo Street in Cusco, attributing it to the Killke culture of about the 13th century, before the Inca — the most direct physical evidence for a 'pre-Inca' origin. The 16th-century Inca chronicler Garcilaso de la Vega recorded in his Royal Commentaries that the Incas themselves admitted many megalithic structures were not their work but found and restored. UC Berkeley's Jean-Pierre Protzen has argued Sacsayhuaman may never have been finished. But whether the finest polygonal masonry belongs to the Inca, the Killke, or an older tradition remains undetermined.

#27 Al Naslaa 阿尔纳斯拉巨石

Location：沙特阿拉伯泰马（Tayma）绿洲以南约 50 公里　Date：地质年代（砂岩地层）；岩画约 4000 年历史；劈裂时间不确定

The Mystery：In the Tayma desert stands a sandstone boulder about 6 meters high and 9 meters wide, split down the middle by a fissure as straight as a knife cut. The two halves each balance on a small natural pedestal and have stood for millennia. The fissure is so straight and smooth it hardly looks natural — the rock is one of the birthplaces of the viral 'alien laser cut' claim. Its south-east face is covered with petroglyphs some 4,000 years old (horses, ibex and more), showing that ancient people already marveled at this stone. Whether the split is a natural joint widened by weathering or the work of some unknown force remains undetermined.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Geologists consider a natural joint the most likely cause: water seeped into a crack, freeze-thaw cycles widened it year by year, and windblown sand abraded it until the boulder split along the joint plane. The boulder's overall form results from long-term wind erosion and chemical weathering.

Open Controversy：University of Bristol geologist and honorary research fellow Cherry Lewis told the MailOnline that water seeping into cracks and freezing — 'freeze-thaw' weathering — could split the rock over millennia, with wind-blown sand 'sandblasting' the faces smooth; though she conceded the crack looks man-made at first glance. University of Birmingham geologist Tim Reston instead favors a natural joint — the rock splitting along joint planes as pressure released. The 'laser cut' and 'alien' claims circulate only as anonymous social-media jokes, with no named scholar behind them.

#28 Atacama Giant 阿塔卡马巨人

Location：智利塔拉帕卡大区，阿塔卡马沙漠塞罗乌尼塔山（Cerro Unita），伊基克东北约85公里　Date：约公元1000–1400年（前哥伦布时期，安第斯文化）

The Mystery：When people think of South American geoglyphs, only Nazca comes to mind. But on the Cerro Unita hillside in Chile's Atacama Desert stands a human figure some 119 meters tall — the largest prehistoric anthropomorphic geoglyph known, far bigger than any single Nazca figure. It is just one of nearly 5,000 geoglyphs in the Tarapacá region: South America's geoglyph tradition extends far beyond Nazca, from the Atacama to Pintados, the whole desert a canvas. The giant has a square head and long limbs, with a striped crown rising above; it was made by subtraction — scraping away dark oxidized topsoil to reveal pale sand, edged with stones. From the ground only scattered stones are visible; the figure emerges only from above. A thousand years ago, who carved this giant on a desert hillside — a god, a calendar, a signpost? Still undecided.

Mainstream Archaeological View：Mainstream archaeology attributes it to Andean cultures of 1000–1400 CE (under Tiwanaku and Inca influence), part of a tradition of nearly 5,000 geoglyphs in the Tarapacá region; the figure may represent a shaman (yatiri) or an Andean deity, serving ritual, astronomical, or caravan-navigation purposes. Chile's Ministry of National Assets declared it a Protected National Asset in 2020.

Open Controversy：On the "largest" claim: Leiden University archaeologist Karsen Lambers wrote in A Human Environment (2020) that it is "the biggest human figure known to have been produced in prehistory," with the 119-metre measurement confirmed by Chilean authorities. On purpose, three camps: first, the lunar-calendar reading — the headdress points track moonrise/moonset to mark seasons — though no named scholar has argued it systematically in a monograph or paper, and it circulates mainly as popular lore; second, Chilean archaeologist and social anthropologist Gonzalo Pimentel (president of the Fundación Desierto de Atacama) holds that the geoglyphs, giant included, are "accurate maps leading to the Pacific Ocean," signposts and memory records for llama caravans crossing the desert; third, the deity reading — local legend identifies the figure as Tunupa-Tarapacá, the Andean thunder-lightning god who journeyed from Lake Titicaca to the Pacific. As for the "alien antennae" claim about the headdress stripes, no named scholar advocates it; it is internet lore.

#29 Kailasa Temple, Ellora 埃洛拉凯拉萨神庙

Location：印度马哈拉施特拉邦　Date：约公元 8 世纪

The Mystery：Cave 16 of the Ellora complex, the Kailasa Temple was carved top-down out of a single basalt cliff — the largest monolithic rock-cut structure in the world. Legend credits 7,000 artisans laboring for 150 years, yet at that pace several tons of rock would have had to be cut and hauled away every single day, leaving the logistics and the true schedule an open question. More fundamentally, no design drawings or construction records survive — and top-down carving allows no second chances. How the precision of its towers, courtyards, and elephant sculptures was guaranteed remains unexplained.

Mainstream Archaeological View：A Hindu cave temple dedicated to Shiva, commissioned under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (c. 8th century CE) and executed in the traditional Indian top-down rock-cut technique by generations of artisans.

Open Controversy：Mainstream archaeology attributes the temple to Krishna I of the 8th-century Rashtrakuta dynasty, but the '7,000 artisans, 150 years' figures come only from legend. In 2026, Indian filmmaker Shekhar Kapur (director of Mr. India) publicly questioned on social media how artisans with only hammers and chisels achieved zero-error carving in a top-down, no-undo excavation, calling it 'a mystery greater than the mystery of the Pyramids of Giza.' As for 'alien builders,' no named scholar or institution advocates it — it circulates as internet-media speculation.

#30 Mohenjo-daro 摩亨佐-达罗

Location：巴基斯坦信德省　Date：约公元前2600–1900年

The Mystery：Mohenjo-daro was one of the largest cities of the Indus Valley Civilization, with a grid of planned streets, a city-wide drainage system, and the famous Great Bath — urban engineering millennia ahead of its time. Yet this highly organized city left no palaces, temples, royal tombs, or images of rulers: a ‘civilization without kings’ whose power structure remains unknown. Thousands of seals bear a script no one has deciphered. Around 1900 BCE the thriving city went into sudden decline and its people left, for reasons still unsettled. (Sister sites: Harappa, Dholavira and Rakhigarhi in India.)

Mainstream Archaeological View：Mainstream archaeology views Mohenjo-daro as a major urban center of the mature Harappan phase, its planning and sanitation reflecting a highly developed social organization; the decline is more likely linked to river shifts, aridification, and the breakdown of trade networks than to foreign invasion.

Open Controversy：In the mid-20th century, British archaeologist Mortimer Wheeler, then Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India, claimed in 'Harappa 1946' that skeletons at Mohenjo-daro evidenced an Aryan-invader 'massacre,' writing 'on circumstantial evidence, Indra stands accused.' In 1964, UC Berkeley's George Dales published 'The Mythical Massacre at Mohenjodaro' in Expedition, showing the skeletons came from different strata and could not reflect a single event — the 'massacre' was myth. In 2019, Vasant Shinde's Deccan College team, sequencing ancient DNA from Rakhigarhi, confirmed the Harappans were an indigenous population. Who governed the city and how power worked remains undetermined, and the Indus script is still undeciphered — decipherment proposals, including Finnish Indologist Asko Parpola's, have not won scholarly acceptance.

#31 Pyramids Around the World 世界金字塔之谜

Location：埃及吉萨为主，兼及墨西哥、秘鲁、波斯尼亚、南极　Date：约公元前2600年起（吉萨大金字塔约公元前2560年）

The Mystery：On Egypt's Giza plateau, the three great pyramids of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure have stood for over 4,500 years — the Great Pyramid built from some 2.3 million stone blocks with astonishing cardinal precision. But what truly unsettles: pyramids do not belong to Egypt alone. The Pyramid of the Sun at Teotihuacan, Kukulkan's pyramid at Chichen Itza, Caral in Peru (among the oldest pyramid complexes in the Americas), the 'pyramid hills' of Visočica in Bosnia — even a pyramid-shaped peak in Antarctica's Ellsworth Mountains. Ancient civilizations separated by oceans, with no contact between them, all raised the same shape. Coincidence, independent invention — or some lost connection?

Mainstream Archaeological View：Mainstream archaeology holds that the pyramid — wide at the base, narrow at the top — is the most stable shape for piled stone, so independent invention around the world is unsurprising; symbolically it stands for the sacred mountain and the axis of the world, a universal language of reaching heaven. For Giza specifically, the mainstream account represented by American Egyptologist Mark Lehner and Egyptian archaeologist Zahi Hawass is that tens of thousands of craftsmen and seasonal laborers moved the blocks with ramps, sledges, levers and Nile transport — the pyramid as the pharaoh's tomb and 'resurrection machine'. Bosnia's 'pyramids' were shown by field geologists to be natural flatiron landforms; Antarctica's 'pyramid' is a glacial horn (nunatak), its formation publicly explained by several geologists.

Open Controversy：The Orion Correlation Theory: Belgian-Egyptian writer and engineer Robert Bauval first published it in 1989 and expanded it in 'The Orion Mystery' (1994, with Adrian Gilbert), arguing the three Giza pyramids mirror the three belt stars of Orion — a celestial blueprint on the ground; astronomer Edwin Krupp and others objected over mismatches in angle and orientation. The lost-civilization diffusion claim: British writer Graham Hancock argues an advanced Ice Age civilization spread pyramid-building worldwide The Bosnian pyramid claim: Bosnian-American businessman Semir Osmanagić has claimed since 2005 that the Visočica hills are the largest man-made pyramids on Earth; the European Association of Archaeologists denounced it as a 'cruel hoax', geologists identified natural flatiron formations, and Zahi Hawass called the theory 'pure hallucination'. Hyperdiffusionism: early-20th-century Australian anatomist Grafton Elliot Smith held that civilization arose once, in Egypt, and everything including pyramids diffused from there — a theory mainstream anthropology has abandoned. The Antarctic 'pyramid': no named scholar supports an artificial origin; Prof. Eric Rignot (UC Irvine) and Mitch Darcy (German Research Centre for Geosciences) have both stated publicly it is simply a pyramid-shaped mountain (nunatak) carved by glacial erosion.
